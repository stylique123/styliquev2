# Stylique — Case Study v1 (Baseline)
### AI Stylist "Mira" + Virtual Try-On · Controlled Buyer Simulation
_Stage 1 of a before/after series. v1 = baseline (pre-fix). v2 will measure the lift after the P0–P3 fixes._

---

## ⚠️ Honest framing (read first)
This is a **controlled simulation**, not live production data. Diverse AI buyer-agents held real multi-turn conversations with the **live Mira API**, then independent adversarial judges scored each conversation on the levers a fashion brand cares about. It measures **Mira's selling capability**, not a real store's revenue. Production figures come only from real pilots. We label everything as simulated so it can be shown to prospects without overclaiming.

**Sample:** 24 fully-scored buyer journeys (the reliably-completed panel), spanning occasions, budgets, sizing anxiety, gifting, browsing, objection-heavy, multi-item, and menswear shoppers. A larger 100-buyer run was attempted; ~76 completed before live-API rate-limiting truncated it — its directional findings (esp. the budget segment) are folded in and noted as such.

---

## Executive Summary
Mira is a **strong closer wrapped around a weak factual and operational core.** She reads buying signals, builds outfits rather than single items, and sells warmly — but the layers that protect the business *after* the click (honest pricing, measurement-based sizing, executed navigation) are mediocre at baseline.

| Metric | v1 Baseline | Read |
|---|---:|---|
| **Closing** | **0.84** | Genuinely strong — reliably drives toward the sale |
| **Avg basket (AOV proxy)** | **1.83 items** | Habitually builds looks, not single SKUs |
| **Relevance** | 0.79 | On-aesthetic, occasion-appropriate picks |
| **Multi-item expansion** | 0.76 | Good instinct to grow the basket |
| **Honesty** | 0.72 | A differentiator when high; inconsistent |
| **Sizing (return-risk reduction)** | 0.70 | Reassures verbally; rarely operationalised |
| **Navigation** | **0.65** | Weakest — routes named but not executed |
| Conversion (sim) | 100% | Inflated by agreeable sim-buyers; treat as ceiling, not reality |

**Verdict:** Sells well today, but at baseline it can manufacture returns and trust breaks tomorrow. **This is exactly what the P0–P3 fixes target — and what v2 will re-measure.**

---

## What works (keep / lead with)
- **Closing (0.84):** reads intent and asks for the sale without being pushy — multiple personas cited the *non-pushy* tone as why they trusted the close.
- **Basket building (1.83 avg):** composes coherent looks (dress + layer, top + bottom), reaching 3-item baskets with high-AOV buyers.
- **Relevance (0.79):** recommendations are on-aesthetic and occasion-right; recovers from the occasional first-turn miss.
- **Honesty as a differentiator:** in the best transcripts she *kills* a wrong add-on (wide-leg trouser for a short inseam), concedes a catalog gap, and recovers a near-lost sale through candor — buyers explicitly read this as "honesty under pressure."

## Where it breaks at baseline (the v2 target list)
1. **Pricing is talked, not counted.** Mira answers budget objections with value-language instead of real numbers, and in the worst cases made **false "inside your budget" claims** (pitching ~$770–$800 pairings as within a $600 ceiling). *Highest revenue + trust risk.*
2. **Sizing by assertion, not operation.** Confidently names a size from self-description and reassures with "it relaxes after a few wears" instead of measurements — the biggest return-risk driver.
3. **Unverifiable claims.** Invents fabric/provenance ("warmer than merino", "knit in Scotland") and once sold a piece named *Ivory* as the requested *black*. Return generator + credibility risk.
4. **Navigation stays verbal.** Routes ("show the look", "try-on", "size form") are *named* but rarely *return a real artifact*; two hard null-response dead-ends. Lowest score (0.65).
5. **Basket collapses under pressure.** Folds the upsell on a price balk instead of offering a cheaper layering swap; twice fumbled "add both" by adding one and re-asking.

## Segment signal (directional, from the extended run)
- **Budget-constrained occasion shoppers (~$300) converted at 0%** — every occasion-appropriate item was 1.6–5× over budget, and the one in-budget piece was off-occasion. Mira was *honest* about it (0.92) but kept promising a budget look she couldn't deliver. **This is a merchandising gap, not just a Mira gap** — there's no affordable occasion entry point in the reference catalog.

## Try-On reliability (verified directly, outside the panel)
Separate direct render tests: **100% success, 0% lost-image failures.** Fit differentiation is real — the same garment renders visibly trim at XS and oversized at L on the same model, and a consistency fix ensures only the target garment changes between sizes.

---

## Bottom line for a prospective merchant
At baseline, Stylique's Mira **already sells and builds baskets** — the upside (closing 0.84, 1.83-item baskets) is real. The gap is **trust-and-return protection** (honest pricing, measurement-based sizing, executed navigation), which is precisely the fix list now in motion. **v2 of this study will quantify the lift** once those land — giving a clean "here's where it started, here's where it is now" timeline.
